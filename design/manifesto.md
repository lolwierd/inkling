# Inkling's manifesto

The rules Inkling is built on. A feature that breaks one of them doesn't go in.

1. You work out what you think by writing it. On the page the numbers have to add up and each
   step has to follow from the last, and that only helps if the sentences are yours. So every
   sentence in a note is one you typed. Inkling doesn't finish, rewrite, draft or summarise
   into your note, and a correction has no Apply button: you make the fix.

2. A good question in the right place is the most useful thing a reader can give you. Inkling
   asks next to the exact words it's about. When a fact is plainly wrong it says so and shows
   how it knows, with a sum from your own numbers or your own words from elsewhere in the
   note. If it can't show that, it asks instead.

3. Most of what you write needs no comment, and a reader who comments on everything gets
   ignored. Inkling marks each paragraph it has read, so its silence means the paragraph held
   up. It waits for you to pause, and it only speaks in the margin of the page you're on. No
   notifications, reminders or badges.

4. The column is for your words. Inkling's go in the margin, and hiding the threads gives you
   the page exactly as you wrote it. Nothing Inkling says ends up in the body of a note unless
   you put it there.

5. A question about your thinking belongs on a thread. It hangs on particular words, so you
   can check it against them, and it stays open until it's settled, so the open threads on a
   page are what you haven't worked out yet. A chat about the whole note has no anchor and no
   end, so chat stays at the side.

6. The best answer is often a better sentence. When you change the words a thread points at,
   Inkling reads them again, and if the problem is gone the thread ties off. What you work out
   in the margin belongs in the note, because the note is what's left when the margin is
   hidden.

7. Keeping your words is an answer too. If you've weighed a question and decided your
   sentence stands, Inkling remembers that, and no mind raises the point again. It keeps no
   score: no streaks, no count of what it caught, nothing that rewards you for agreeing with it.

8. Everything Inkling keeps about you or a note is somewhere you can read it: the note's
   brief, what it has learned about you, and every thread it has opened, including the ones
   you cut. You'll only write half-formed thoughts in a notebook if you know what its reader
   keeps.
