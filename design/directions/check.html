Direction 2 of 3 · Engineering

Chk'd: Inkling as the checker

Inkling is the checker. It goes over your note in yellow for what holds and red for what doesn’t, and when every red mark is answered, it initials the page.

Modelled on an engineer’s computation pad

The grid is printed on the back. Engineering paper is pale green with its grid on the reverse, so it shows through, survives erasing, and doesn’t photocopy.

Here it lives in the margins and fades out behind your words.

The checker’s colours. Yellow over everything verified, red for anything that must change, another colour for notes that aren’t changes.

Here: yellow holds, red is a check, blue is conversation.

The title block. By, Date, Chk’d, Date. A sheet isn’t done until the checker initials it, and a revision gets a cloud and a Δ.

Here it is the note’s header, and the sign-off you earn.

Sheet06of 13

Colour

Every colour has one job, taken from the checker’s pencils. Contrast is measured on this page against the sheet each colour sits on: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for marks.

Yellow: verified. Read and holds, or answered. Bars, swipes, filled tags.

Red: must change. Rings, underlines, open tags, leaders. Never a fill, never a sentence.

Blue: conversation. Your replies’ answers, Ask the room, chat.

Sheet08of 13

Motion and sound

Motion is the checker’s hand: it marks, it never decorates, and it never runs on the words you are typing. Checks arrive in silence; only finishing makes a sound.

Swipe · a verified check. Filtered noise rising from 1.1 to 2.6 kHz over 220 ms, like a highlighter crossing paper.

Stamp · the page is signed. A 140 Hz thump falling to 55 Hz, with a short tick on top.

No sound · when a new check lands. A check should never interrupt you twice: once to see it, once to hear it.

Silent

Sound is off by default in the app and on this page. Pressing Play turns it on.

What it refuses, and its risk

A direction is mostly the things it will not do.

Refuses

01

Grid under your words. The grid fades out at the edge of the text column. Texture under text costs every reader a little, every line.

02

Red as a fill, or red sentences. Red rings and tags only. A paragraph of red text reads as an alarm, and Inkling is asking, not alarming.

03

All-caps reading text. Drafting letters in capitals; notes are not drawings. Capitals stay in 12 px labels.

04

Fake precision. No made-up measurements, gauges or numbers dressed as data. Every number on the sheet is yours or Inkling’s arithmetic.

05

Initials it hasn’t earned. The Chk’d cell fills only when nothing is open, and empties the moment you change checked words.

Risk

!

It can feel technical for journaling. A diary entry with a title block can feel like paperwork.

→

Atkinson’s open shapes carry the words, not a technical face; B612 appears only in Inkling’s notes and labels.

→

Every mark is drawn by hand: rings wobble and overshoot, ticks taper, initials are written. Nothing is a vector checkbox.

→

The title block can fold to one line (note, Chk’d, open) for notes that aren’t plans. The sign-off still happens.

→

Plain, friendly words on every control, and the drafting words only in status lines.

Sheet12of 13

Things you might have missed

Specific to Chk’d. The cross-cutting ones (app icon variants for macOS 26, Dock badge, VoiceOver) are on the overview.

01

Red on a green pad is the classic colour-blind trap. For deuteranopes the red rings and the green sheet sit close in hue. They still work, because the rings are dark against a light sheet and carry a number; that is why no check is ever colour alone.

02

What edits do to initials. Change checked words and the Chk’d cell clears, a Δ goes in Rev, and only the clouded part is re-checked. Initials never sit on words they didn’t see.

03

The note list is a status board. ✓, a number, or Δ beside every note, and “Open checks” as a filter in ⌘K, so “what’s unfinished?” is one keystroke.

04

Long notes sign by section. A note with headings gets a Chk’d mark per heading, so one open check at the bottom doesn’t hold the whole page hostage.

05

Whose initials. IK for Inkling; a custom mind takes its own two letters, so a note checked by “Skeptic” reads SK.

06

Export keeps the block if you ask. Word and PDF can carry the title block as a header (By, Date, Chk’d), which is what a design doc for a team wants. Off by default; the marks never print.

07

Blueprint is a real dark mode, not inverted green. The yellow stays the same, because verified should not change meaning at night.
