Inkling · Loose Threads · after the pick

Loose Threads, in motion

Three things the pick left open: how the threads move, how a diagram is sewn, and how code sits in a note. Everything below runs. Under each motion is its timing, drawn as a ruler you can drag; the bar above can slow it all down or show the reduced-motion version.

01

Motion

Motion is how Inkling’s marks behave like thread: they sag, pull tight, knot and drop. It is never how your words behave. Every motion here is a function of the time since it started, which is how the app already animates (anim_since on an Instant), so what you scrub on this page ports to a GPUI canvas as it is.

1.1

Tags stay folded until you look

A thread’s tag sits folded on its thread: the mind’s mark, its name and the thread’s number, nothing else. Rest the pointer on it and it drops open to show the question; click and it opens for a reply. It folds again when you move away, press Esc or start typing. Drop is the pick; the other three ways it could open are here too, on the same note, to compare.

Rest the pointer on a tag to read it; click to reply; Esc folds it.
Duration
Full height in about 200 ms, 4% past it at 260, settled by 380. Opening for a reply runs the same spring again. Folding takes about 250 ms.
Easing
Height: a spring with a little give, stiffness 300, damping 25 (ζ 0.72). Width: 700 / 53, no bounce, and it leads. Folding: 700 / 53 for both, the width 60 ms behind the height.
What moves
The label’s width and height, out from its loop; the thread, from 26% to 60% (read) or 100% (open); tags below step down. Nothing is drawn at the edge. The header row, the loop and your words never move.
Hover and click
The pointer rests 120 ms before anything drops, so passing over the margin opens nothing; leaving folds it 250 ms later. Click or Enter opens it and puts the caret in the reply. Esc, a click elsewhere or a key in the note folds it. One tag open at a time.
Reduced
No drop: the label is drawn at its new size and fades in over 120 ms.
GPUI
The card’s body sits in a div with overflow_hidden; with_spring drives its width and height between the folded and measured sizes (margin::measured already records heights). Nothing else is drawn. Springs keep their velocity when retargeted, so leaving mid-drop folds back from where it is. GPUI cannot rotate or skew a div with text in it, so none of the four flips or tilts the label. S.

The four, frame by frame: folded to read

The pick: Drop

  1. It is the cleanest. Nothing is drawn: no crease, no roll, no stitching at the edge. The label falls open from the loop it hangs by, and the only thing that changes is its size.
  2. It is the thread’s own physics. A tag hanging on a thread finds its length with a small give, the same give the rope has when it sags. The label and its thread move as one object.
  3. Nothing you read moves. The header row and the loop stay put, and the question is uncovered where it will rest, so the first line can be read while the rest is still dropping.
  4. A folded tag still says it opens, without a bar. It answers the pointer at once: the label lifts a little, 120 ms before anything drops. Its thread stays still: no sway on hover.
  5. It is the cheapest to build. One clipped div and two springs; no gradient, no extra element.

Why not the others

  1. Unfold draws its crease as a strip along the bottom edge. On a tag at rest it looked out of place, and it is the fussiest of the four to draw.
  2. Unroll reads well, but a roll is paper, a scroll, not a woven label.
  3. Stitch round is the loveliest once and the slowest every other time (about 700 ms before you can read). It belongs to a thread’s first arrival, not to every hover.

Rules every motion keeps

  • Nothing moves while you type.A key press finishes every margin animation at its end. New threads arrive without motion, the needle holds still, and layout shifts re-hang threads without a swing.
  • Thread moves; words don’t.Stitches, threads, knots and tags move. Your text never slides, scales or fades, and nothing reflows mid-motion.
  • Open slower than close.Opening settles in about 300 ms, closing in about 250, and a press or a hover answers in under 120.
  • Springs where you can change your mind.Hover, drag and panels are springs that keep their speed when retargeted. Tie-off and snip are fixed timelines.
  • Reduced motion is a fade.Every draw-on becomes a 120 ms fade, physics jumps to rest, and nothing sways.
02

Diagrams

Inkling already sketches in steps: each step a group with a caption, drawn after the last. A flowchart is the same sketch with a stitch key. Every shape is something you could sew, so a diagram looks made on this page, not pasted in from a drawing tool. Whose diagram it is shows in the thread: Inkling sketches in madder; a diagram you make, or one you drag into your note, is sewn in your ink.

The stitch key

Four diagrams, sewn step by step

GPUI. drawing.rs already parses stepped SVG and paints it step by step (STEP_EVERY 1.35 s, STEP_DRAW 0.75 s). Ask the model for a data-kind on each shape (step, start, end, decision, flow, back, group, lane, label, chosen) and paint each kind with a stitch.rs primitive: a patch is a paint_quad with running_stitch along its inset edge; a decision is cross_stitch along four edges; flow is running_stitch along the polyline the parser already flattens; a way back is paint_rope’s thread plus short tacking stitches; a hoop is two rounded quads and a clamp; a seam is a rule and a running stitch. Within a step, stitches appear in path order from the same progress value the playback already has. Labels are text runs on a card-coloured quad. Cost: M.
03

Code

Code is the one place a note is allowed a grid. A sampler’s alphabet is worked on even-weave, every letter the same number of threads wide, and code is set the same way: monospace on a flat ground. The block itself stays plain: code is read one character at a time, so nothing is sewn into it except, in madder, Inkling’s question about one of its lines.

Ground
The app’s code colour, flat, 8 px corners: the same radius as a tag, so the page has one family of corners.
Edge
None. The ground is a step darker than the page in linen and a step lighter in lamplight; a rule around it would be a second edge doing the first one’s job.
Label
The language, top left, Figtree 12 px in faint. Copy appears on hover or focus, top right, a plain button.
Line numbers
Spline Sans Mono 12 px in faint, right-aligned in 40 px, tabular. Not selectable, so a copy is only the code.
Long lines
Wrap rather than scroll: a note is read, not edited like a file. The rest of the line hangs two characters past its own indentation and gets no number.
Inline code
The same face at 0.86 of the note, on a 6% ink chip with 4 px corners. Nothing else changes, so it sits in a Fraunces sentence without shouting.
A thread on a line
The running stitch goes under the exact characters asked about, in the line’s leading; the line takes the madder wash while its tag is unfolded; the knot sits in the note’s gutter, beside the line number.

The font: Spline Sans Mono

Syntax colours, from the thread box

Five threads, two greys and ink. Madder (and rose, its neighbour) is never used: a madder mark in a code block always means Inkling. Sage and ochre are taken 12% towards ink so they read at 4.5:1 on the code ground; the ratios are measured on this page.

GPUI. A code block is a new BlockKind::Code(lang) over a run of lines: one rounded quad in p.code behind them, numbers shaped and painted in the gutter the way numbered lists already are, Spline Sans Mono bundled as two static instances (400, 500) with FontFeatures zero on and calt off. Colours are TextRuns from a small per-language lexer (keywords, strings, numbers, comments, types: S to M; tree-sitter would be L and heavy). A long line wraps to two characters past its own indentation, with no number. Inline code is Mark::Code, which today uses Menlo: switch the family, size it at 0.86 of the note. A thread on a code line needs nothing new: anchors are character ranges, so the running stitch already follows the line box, and the hole sits 7 px right of the block.
04

Other blocks

Your blocks can be sewn too. What tells yours from Inkling’s is colour, not stitching: madder, and rose beside it, is only ever Inkling’s. A block is sewn in your ink or in the thread of its kind, and its words stay in Fraunces. Buttons and toggles in the app’s chrome stay plain; anything inside your note is yours to sew, checkboxes included. Each block has two or three ways to sew it, live, and the one recommended goes into the note at the end.
